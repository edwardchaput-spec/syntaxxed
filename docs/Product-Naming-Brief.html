What are we actually naming?

A plain-English guide to the product, the problem it solves and the idea a great name should capture. No coding knowledge required.

Audience: family naming workshop Working name: Syntaxxed Goal: short, clever and credible

The quick version

AI coding assistants are useful, but they need to see parts of a software project to help. Giving them the whole project can be wasteful, confusing and risky. This product prepares only the useful parts first.

It gives an AI assistant the right code, at the right level of detail, while keeping the developer in control of what is shared.
A
Think of it like packing a specialist's tool case.
You do not empty the entire workshop into the van. You understand the job, choose the tools that matter, remove anything private, then check the case before it leaves.

It is not another AI chatbot

The product does not try to replace ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Cursor or any other coding assistant. It sits before them and improves what they are allowed to see.

The problem

1

Too much noise

An entire codebase contains files that have nothing to do with the current task. Extra material can distract the AI and weaken its answer.

2

Too much exposure

Projects can contain credentials, internal details and commercially sensitive code that should not be shared casually.

3

Too little visibility

Developers often cannot easily see the exact package of information an AI tool is about to receive.

4

Too little control

Most tools jump from “no access” to “read everything”. There is rarely a sensible middle ground with progressive permission.

The product solves a balancing problem: an AI needs enough useful context to help, but it should not automatically receive everything available.

How it works

Understand the taskThe developer describes what they are trying to change or investigate.
Find relevant filesThe product suggests the parts of the project most likely to matter.
Choose the depthThe developer decides whether the AI needs an outline, working logic or full source.
Protect and prepareObvious secrets are hidden and unnecessary content is reduced.
Preview and shareThe developer reviews the exact result before copying or approving access.

Three useful levels of detail

Lowest disclosureOutline

The shape of the code: names, types, structure and available functions, without the working implementation.

Working detailLogic

The important implementation, with comments and avoidable bulk removed.

Highest disclosureSource

The complete selected file, still checked for recognisable secrets before sharing.

Key features

Task-focused selection

Finds the files most likely to help with the job instead of treating every file as equally important.

Progressive access

Shares the minimum useful level first, then allows deeper detail only when it is genuinely needed.

Visible preview

Shows the developer exactly what the AI will receive before anything is copied or approved.

Secret redaction

Recognises and hides many common credentials and private keys. It is a safety layer, not a promise of perfect detection.

Local-first preparation

Ordinary scanning and preparation happen on the developer's computer rather than uploading the repository to this product.

Less wasted context

Reduces irrelevant material, which can lower AI usage costs and make the assistant's attention more focused.

Works across AI tools

The idea is vendor-neutral. It can support different assistants rather than belonging to one model company.

Optional agent gateway

A compatible AI agent can request one file at a time and ask for deeper access, with the developer approving sensitive levels.

What makes it different

The central USP

Most AI coding products focus on what the AI can do after it sees the code. This product focuses on controlling and improving what the AI sees in the first place.

  • Control before disclosure: selection, protection and review happen before the AI boundary.
  • A useful middle ground: it is not simply “block access” or “share the whole repository”.
  • Progressive trust: an assistant can start with the shape of the code and request more only when necessary.
  • Developer ownership: the human can see, adjust and approve the context rather than surrendering control invisibly.
  • Local and vendor-neutral: the same approach can sit in front of several AI tools without becoming another chatbot.

The emotional promise

The user should feel in control, clear-headed and protected— not frightened, restricted or buried in security language. The product should feel like a capable assistant that prepares the right material and keeps the owner in charge.

Where it can grow

Today it is a free tool for individual developers. The larger opportunity is a trusted context gateway for teams and enterprises.

A strong name must therefore work for a friendly solo-developer utility now and a serious business control layer later. It should not trap the product inside one editor, one AI company or the narrow idea of saving tokens.

The naming brief

A good name should

  • Be short enough to say, type and remember easily—roughly 4 to 10 letters is ideal, although a brilliant name can stretch slightly.
  • Sound natural when spoken: “Install ___”, “Run ___”, “Our company uses ___”.
  • Have a logical connection to control, context, relevance, boundaries, ownership or trusted access.
  • Feel approachable to an individual developer but credible in an enterprise sales meeting.
  • Leave room for the product to grow from a tool into a platform or gateway.
  • Be distinctive enough to own as a brand, company, domain and software package after proper checks.
  • Reward a second look: a clean portmanteau, double meaning or subtle pun is welcome if the word still sounds believable.

Avoid names that

  • Sound destructive, such as chopping, killing or stripping code.
  • Make the product sound like only a compressor, token saver or file-shortening utility.
  • Suggest it is a programming-language parser, formatter or syntax checker.
  • Sound like a generic AI chatbot by adding “AI” to an ordinary noun.
  • Promise perfect security or make the product sound like surveillance software.
  • Depend on awkward spelling, unexplained punctuation or a pronunciation people will debate.
  • Tie the brand to VS Code, MCP, one model company or another implementation detail that may change.

Creative territories

These are ingredients, not name suggestions. Mix ideas from different territories, bend them, shorten them and look for double meanings.

Ownership & agency

The developer remains in charge of their repository, choices and permissions.

Frame & boundary

The product creates a deliberate view or safe perimeter around what the AI can see.

Signal & relevance

Useful information is separated from noise so attention lands in the right place.

Layers & disclosure

Information is revealed progressively, from shape to logic to full source.

Gate & passage

The product is a controlled crossing point between private code and an outside assistant.

Pack & preparation

The right material is selected, checked and assembled for a particular job.

Lens & focus

The product narrows a huge codebase into the most useful view for the task.

Trust & custody

Private material is handled deliberately, visibly and with accountable approval.

Repository & source

The language can hint at code, repos, branches or source without becoming overly technical.

Workshop prompt

A simple 35-minute naming session

  1. Read only the quick version, USP and naming brief.
  2. Work separately for 10 minutes. Each person writes at least 15 names—quantity first, no judging.
  3. Share every name without defending it. Circle anything that creates an immediate reaction.
  4. Combine or mutate the best fragments for another 10 minutes.
  5. Score the final shortlist. Domain, company and trademark research comes after the creative work.
Name the product that gives an AI assistant the right code, at the right level of detail, while keeping the developer visibly in control. It begins as a useful individual tool and can grow into the trusted gateway between company code and every AI agent.

This is a creative brief, not legal clearance. Any shortlisted name still needs live domain, company, software marketplace and similar-trademark checks.
